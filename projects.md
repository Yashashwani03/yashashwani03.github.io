<h3> Probing Thermodynamic Compressibility of low dimensional systems using Quantum Capacitance measurements </h3>
<p>
  When we talk about low-dimensional systems, they have a finite density of states. This just means that, as opposed to metals, where when I change the number of charge carriers, the fermi level is not affected, for lo-dimensional systems like graphene, upon changing the carrier concentration, we see a shift in the chemical potential as well! This shift in the chemical potential as a change in the carrier concentration or DOS, is modelled as a capacitance, and is called Quantum Capacitance.

  As part of my master's project, I measured the capacitance response of Bilayer and Trilayer graphene systems. My master's thesis can be accessed here. 



</p>
<h3> Superconductivity: A sneak-peek into the Bardeen-Cooper-Schreiffer Theory </h3>
<p>
  The brain child of Bardeen, Cooper and Schrieffer, the BCS theory of superconductivity explains the underlying physics of what we now know as Conventional Superconductors. These systems are unstable towards the formation of eletron-electron pairs, forming a condensate of Cooper pairs, gapped from the normal state that houses individual electrons, via the superconducting gap. 
  This theory was the first to provide a comprehensive description of what actually goes on inside a superconductor.
